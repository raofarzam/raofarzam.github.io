Off-page SEO for small businesses: how link building really works

Rao Farzam Ahmed21 November 2026, 7 min read

Off-page SEO is everything that happens away from your own website that helps it rank on Google. The biggest part of it is link building: getting other websites to link to yours. I spent two years doing this for client websites in the UK, US and UAE, and it remains the part of SEO that business owners understand least.

Why links still matter

Think of a link as a recommendation. When a respected website links to yours, it is telling Google: this page is worth visiting. Google has used links as a trust signal since its early days, and they are still one of the strongest signals it has.

But not all links are equal. One link from a well-known local news site or an industry association is worth more than hundreds of links from spammy directories. Google is very good at spotting the difference.

What makes a good link

  1. Relevance. A link to a bakery from a food blog makes sense. A link from a car-parts site doesn’t.
  2. Trust. Links from real, established websites with real visitors carry weight.
  3. Natural placement. A link inside a genuine article or listing, where a reader might actually click it.
  4. Variety. Links from many different sites look natural. Hundreds from one site don’t.

Safe ways a small business can earn links

Business listings and directories

Start with the basics: Google Business Profile, the main business directories in your country and listings in your industry. Keep your name, address and phone number exactly the same everywhere.

Partners, suppliers and associations

The businesses you already work with often have a “partners” or “where to buy” page. Ask them to list you. Trade associations and local chambers of commerce often do the same for members.

Content worth linking to

People link to useful things: a clear guide, a price comparison, a local resource. One genuinely helpful article on your site can earn links for years.

Guest articles and outreach

Writing a useful article for another website in your field, with a link back to yours, still works when it’s done honestly. The key is real value for that site’s readers, not just a link.

What to avoid

Buying cheap link packages (“1,000 backlinks for $10”) is the fastest way to waste money, and it can get a site penalised by Google. The same goes for link swaps on a large scale and private networks of fake blogs. If an offer sounds too fast and too cheap, it is.

The fastest result I’ve had was ranking a US e-commerce client’s target keyword within 15 days. That speed came from a well-built site, a realistic keyword and quality links working together. Links alone rarely do it, and results like that aren’t typical for every keyword.

Where to start this week

  1. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile.
  2. List your business on three trusted directories in your country or industry.
  3. Ask two partners or suppliers to link to your website.
  4. Make sure your own site is indexed first. My guide on getting a new website on Google walks through it.
