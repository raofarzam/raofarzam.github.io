WordPress or a custom-coded website: which one does your business need?

Rao Farzam Ahmed21 November 2026, 6 min read

Almost every business owner who asks me for a website eventually asks the same question: should it be WordPress, or built from scratch? I build both, and the honest answer is that it depends on what your business needs the website to do.

What each one is

WordPress is a content management system that powers a large share of the world’s websites. You choose a theme, add plugins for extra features, and edit pages through a dashboard, much like writing a document.

A custom-coded website is written by a developer, usually in HTML, CSS and JavaScript, sometimes with a framework like React. Every part is built for your site specifically.

Side by side

Editing the content yourself

WordPress wins clearly. If you or your team will add blog posts, products or news every week, WordPress makes that easy without a developer.

Speed

A well-built custom site is usually faster, because it only contains what it needs. WordPress can be fast too, but too many plugins and heavy themes slow it down.

Cost

For a standard business website, WordPress is usually cheaper and quicker to launch. Custom work costs more upfront because everything is built by hand.

Unique features

If you need something unusual, like a special booking flow, a calculator or integration with your own systems, custom code gives you full control. With WordPress you depend on whatever plugins exist.

SEO

Both can rank very well. What matters more is good structure, fast pages, clear titles and useful content. WordPress SEO plugins make the basics easy for non-developers. On a custom site the developer sets them up directly.

Maintenance

WordPress needs regular updates for the core, theme and plugins, otherwise it can become a security risk. A simple custom site has fewer moving parts to update.

My simple rule of thumb

  1. Choose WordPress if you’ll publish content often, want to edit the site yourself, and need a standard business website or blog. The Food Current, a food magazine I built, is a good example.
  2. Choose custom if speed and a unique design matter most, the content rarely changes, or you need features that don’t exist as plugins. My e-commerce demos like Velocity Cycles are hand-coded for this reason.
  3. Not sure? Start with what the website needs to do for your customers, not the technology. The right choice usually becomes obvious.

Whatever you choose, plan for Google

The platform matters less than what happens after launch. A beautiful website nobody can find doesn’t bring in customers. If you want to see what that takes, read why your new website isn’t on Google yet, or tell me about your project and I’ll recommend the right option.
