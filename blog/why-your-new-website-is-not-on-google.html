Why your new website isn’t on Google yet (and the four steps that fix it)

Rao Farzam Ahmed21 November 2026, 6 min read

You’ve launched your website, typed your business name into Google, and nothing comes up. It’s one of the most common worries I hear, and I had the same moment myself when I published this portfolio. The good news: in most cases nothing is broken. Google simply doesn’t know your site exists yet.

Google finds new pages by following links and by reading lists of pages that site owners give it. A brand-new website has neither, so it can sit unnoticed for weeks. These are the four steps I followed to introduce my own site to Google, and they work for almost any website.

1. Add your site to Google Search Console

Google Search Console is a free tool from Google that shows how your site appears in search. Go to search.google.com/search-console, sign in with a Google account, and choose Add a website.

You’ll see two options: Domain and URL prefix. If you can edit your domain’s DNS settings, Domain covers everything. If you can’t (for example on free hosting like GitHub Pages), choose URL prefix and paste your full address, including https://.

2. Prove the website is yours

Google needs proof that you own the site before it shows you any data. The simplest method is the HTML file: Google gives you a small file to download, you upload it to the main folder of your website, and you press Verify.

A mistake I made myself: I pressed Verify too quickly and got “verification file not found”. If your host takes a minute or two to publish changes, open the file’s address in your browser first. When you can see it, Google can too. And never delete that file later, or the verification is lost.

3. Submit your sitemap

A sitemap is a simple file, usually at yoursite.com/sitemap.xml, that lists every page you want Google to find. WordPress SEO plugins create one automatically. On a hand-built site you can write one yourself.

In Search Console, open Sitemaps, type sitemap.xml and press Submit. Don’t worry if the status first says “Couldn’t fetch”. On new properties that often changes to “Success” within a day or two.

4. Request indexing for your most important page

Paste your homepage address into the search bar at the top of Search Console. It will probably say URL is not on Google, which is normal for a new site. Press Request indexing. Your page is added to a priority queue for Google to visit.

Press it once. Requesting the same page again and again doesn’t move it up the queue.

How long does it take?

For a new site, anything from a few days to a couple of weeks is normal. You can help it along:

  1. Link to your website from places Google already visits: your LinkedIn profile, GitHub, social media bios and business listings.
  2. Make sure every page has a clear title and description that says what the page is about.
  3. Keep the site fast and mobile-friendly. Google checks both.

Showing up is step one. Ranking is the real work.

Once Google knows your site, it will appear when people search your exact name. Showing up for searches like “furniture store in Karachi” or “accountant in Manchester” is a different job. That is where keyword research, good content and links from other websites come in, which I cover in my guide to off-page SEO and link building.
